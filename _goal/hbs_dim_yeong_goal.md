# Yeong (靈) — Goal

The goal is that, even if hon (judgment) and baek (execution) are handed over to AI and robots, yeong (靈) — the dimension of dignity, morality, responsibility, and agency — is never outsourced and humans keep the final right of judgment.

## Objectives
- Clearly define the functions of yeong — dignity, morality, responsibility, agency — and recognize that, unlike hon (cognition) or baek (the body), this is a domain where outsourcing is fundamentally impossible.
- Even in situations where organizations and individuals depend on AI's analysis and reasoning or robots' execution, humans retain the authority to judge whether ultimately to accept, reject, revise, or take responsibility.
- Protect in organizational operation the six values of yeong: the ability to judge for oneself, the attitude of taking responsibility for one's own actions, the sense that understands others' suffering, the value that puts life and dignity first, the strength to ask about meaning rather than efficiency, and the agency to refuse the system's commands.

## Success Criteria
The goal is judged to be achieved when, while actively using AI and robots, the final right of judgment and responsibility remain with humans in important decisions, and humans do not give up the last question (whether to accept, reject, revise, or take responsibility).
