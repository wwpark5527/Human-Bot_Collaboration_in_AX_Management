# Accountability Structure — Goal

The reason this indicator exists in an AX organization is to make the organization able to answer **to whom** ultimate responsibility for an AI output is attributed, not in words but in a figure. The more the range in which AI produces outputs and people use them as they are grows, the more responsibility naturally blurs. This indicator fixes the degree of that blurring as one measurable value.

## Objectives

- **Expose the accountability gap as a figure.** By calculating the rate of AI outputs produced and used by the organization for which a final responsible party is designated, it replaces the statement "an accountability structure exists" with a verifiable value, the designation rate. The undesignated remainder is precisely the size of the accountability gap the organization is bearing.
- **Raise designation to the level of individual identification.** Designation at the department or team level cannot identify a respondent when an accident occurs, so this indicator puts only what is designated by an individual identifier into the numerator. The objective is not to raise the designation rate but to raise the **identifiable** designation rate.
- **Separate prior designation from after-the-fact designation.** Attaching a responsible party retroactively after an output has been made is not an accountability structure but after-the-fact attribution of blame. By calculating the prior-designation ratio alongside it, one distinguishes whether the organization designed responsibility or distributed it after an accident.
- **Look at inter-departmental variance as well.** Even if the company-wide average is high, if a particular department is close to 0% then that department has no accountability structure. By reporting the per-department minimum alongside, the gap hidden behind the average is exposed.

## Success Criteria

This indicator is judged a success when all four of the following are satisfied in each measurement cycle.

1. The company-wide final-responsible-party designation rate is at or above the baseline the organization announced in advance.
2. The **minimum** of the per-department designation rates is at or above the baseline — satisfying only the average is not a success.
3. The share that **prior designation** occupies within all designations is at or above the standard.
4. In sample verification, it is confirmed that the designated responsible party actually holds the authority to demand access to, modification of, or suspension of the output in question. A designation that is a name without authority is not counted as a designation.

If even one of the four conditions is unsatisfied, the indicator is recorded as a failure, and the unsatisfied item and the department in question are recorded alongside.
