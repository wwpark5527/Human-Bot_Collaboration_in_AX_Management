# AH_AB_COLLABORATION — Collaboration between Augmented Humans and Augmented Bots

## Concept Definition
Collaboration between augmented humans and augmented bots is a role-based collaboration structure in which a human's purpose, judgment, and accountability are connected with a bot's analysis, execution, and recording capabilities within common context and governance context to produce safer, more refined, and more meaningful results. This is one of the book's central themes, and the core of the collaboration is complementation, not replacement. If a human treats a bot as merely a simple tool, the bot's potential cannot be fully used; conversely, if a human hands even their own judgment over to the bot, accountability and agency weaken. So this collaboration is not a matter of "who is more capable" but a question of "how to connect each other's strengths."

## Decision Criteria
For this collaboration to work properly, three conditions are required. ① Sharing the same context (common context) — the organization's purpose, work standards, reference materials, formats, prohibitions, and so on must be sufficiently conveyed. ② Collaborating within an accountability structure (governance context) — verifiability, approvability, recordability, and accountability must be in place. ③ Dividing roles while augmenting each other — not a simple division of labor but a relationship in which each makes the other stronger. Judged by whether these three conditions are met together.

## Output
The augmented human gives the bot purpose and standards, and the augmented bot shows the human structure and possibility. Common context lets the two work by the same standards, and governance context makes that collaboration operate safely and accountably. When this collaboration works properly, purpose, standards, materials, judgment, revision, approval, and recording become connected, so the result is not a one-off deliverable but an organizational asset that can be reused in the next task.
