# AUGMENTATION_QUOTIENT — Augmentation Quotient (AQ)

## Concept Definition
The Augmentation Quotient (AQ) is a concept higher than AI Utilization (AIU); it is an index that measures not mere usage volume but "the ability to collaborate with AI." It is expressed as AQ = AIU x Role Alignment x Collaboration x Governance, so that beyond AI utilization it also reflects, as a product, the degree of role-centered use (role alignment), the level of human-bot collaboration (collaboration), and the level of ethics/security/approval compliance (governance). Even for two people with the same AIU, if one only does simple searches while the other carries out problem definition, role design, prompt design, result verification, and AI collaboration, their AQ differs greatly. AQ is the second stage of the AIU→AQ→AHI three-stage measurement system for judging whether someone is an augmented human (AH), and it represents augmentation potential.

## Decision Criteria
It is judged by the fact that, even with the same AIU (usage), the AQ value changes according to the product of role-centered use, level of collaboration, and level of governance compliance. That is, the crux of the judgment is not "how much one uses" but "how well-equipped one is with the ability to collaborate."

## Output
A value produced by the formula AQ = AIU x Role Alignment x Collaboration x Governance. It is the second-stage indicator in the AIU→AQ→AHI three-stage system and subsequently becomes an input to the AHI (Augmented Human Index) calculation.
