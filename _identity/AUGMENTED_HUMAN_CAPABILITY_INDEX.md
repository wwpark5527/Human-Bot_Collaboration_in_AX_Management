# AUGMENTED_HUMAN_CAPABILITY_INDEX — Augmented Human Capability Index (AHCI)

## Concept Definition
The Augmented Human Capability Index (AHCI) is an indicator that shifts the object of augmented-human (AH) evaluation from "the person themselves" to "the human's capability." AHCI is composed of five elements — human augmentation degree + bot cooperation degree + human-bot alignment degree + output fidelity + rate of improvement — and it is not a single simple score but a diagnostic structure that jointly examines whether the human is moving closer to AH, whether the bot is moving closer to BH, whether human and bot are aligning, whether the results are faithful and evidence-based, and whether the relationship improves over time. Where the previously described AHI has the feel of grading an "augmented human," AHCI is a growth- and development-oriented diagnostic concept that looks at "how ready one's capability to work with AI is," and it connects naturally with the HBRM system and the Belbin Korea diagnostic tools.

## Decision Criteria
The purpose of the evaluation is to check whether one can collaborate with AI, verify results, make responsible judgments, and use it in line with organizational standards — and all four of these belong to the domain of capability. It is judged by synthesizing the five diagnostic elements (human augmentation degree, bot cooperation degree, human-bot alignment degree, output fidelity, rate of improvement) into "is this human actually being augmented while working with this bot."

## Output
A diagnostic structure produced by the formula AHCI = human augmentation degree + bot cooperation degree + human-bot alignment degree + output fidelity + rate of improvement. It is a capability-diagnosis result that connects naturally with the HBRM system, and a growth- and development-oriented indicator measurable with the Belbin Korea tools.
