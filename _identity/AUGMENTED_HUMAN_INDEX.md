# AUGMENTED_HUMAN_INDEX — Augmented Human Index (AHI)

## Concept Definition
The Augmented Human Index (AHI) is the final stage of the AIU→AQ→AHI three-stage measurement system; it is an augmentation-realization indicator that judges "whether that person is actually an AH (augmented human)." It is expressed as AHI = f(Performance, Learning, Influence, Innovation, Adaptability), produced as a function of performance, learning speed, influence, innovativeness, and adaptability. Someone who uses AI a lot but cannot make judgments and, with reduced creativity, depends on AI is merely an AI User, not an AH. Conversely, if using AI produces increased problem-solving ability, faster learning, improved decision-making, and improved collaboration ability, that person becomes an AH. In the car analogy, if AQ is driving skill, AHI corresponds to the level of an actual racer.

## Decision Criteria
It is judged by synthesizing the five elements — performance, learning speed, influence, innovativeness, adaptability — as a function to distinguish people who merely use AI a lot (H1, H2) from people who are truly augmented while working with AI (AH). If, with declining judgment and reduced creativity, one depends solely on AI, that is not an AH; if problem-solving ability, learning speed, decision-making, and collaboration ability improve together, it is judged to be an AH.

## Output
A value produced by the formula AHI = f(Performance, Learning, Influence, Innovation, Adaptability). It is the final-stage indicator of the AIU→AQ→AHI three-stage system and the final judgment result on "whether one is actually an AH."
