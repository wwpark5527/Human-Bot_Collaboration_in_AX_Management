# COL_PROFESSIONAL_GROUP — Professional-group resistance

## Concept Definition
Resistance waged by highly skilled professional groups on the grounds that their expertise and authority of judgment are being infringed. As the second of the 'collective human reaction/resistance types (5 types),' unlike labor-union-type resistance, it is resistance in which professional groups themselves—such as the medical, education, and legal professions—act as the subject rather than an organized labor union.

## Decision Criteria
Whether the subject of the resistance is a highly skilled profession such as the medical, education, or legal field, and the issues at stake are unclear allocation of responsibility, infringement of expertise, and devaluation of professional worth.

## Output
It appears as concern over the worsening of medical judgment authority, the degradation of task meaning and the nullification of evaluation, and the raising of legal-liability questions.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "Professional-group resistance: highly skilled professionals also resist; examples include the medical field (concern over AI diagnostic systems: unclear allocation of responsibility, infringement of expertise, worsening of medical judgment authority), the education field (teachers' and professors' resistance to generative AI: worsening of task meaning, nullification of evaluation, damage to the value of human education), and the legal field (lawyers' concern over AI legal-drafting tools: devaluation of professional worth, legal-liability issues)."
