# CONTEXT_JUSTICE — Context Justice

## Concept Definition
Context Justice is a principle of justice that, when AI participates in important judgments and outputs, makes the whole membership—rather than only a select few monopolizing the organization's purpose, standards, materials, and verification system—able to access the common production base for working together with AI. It does not view fairness in the AI era merely as a matter of algorithmic bias. More fundamentally, it reframes it as questions of who can give context to AI, who can verify AI results, and who can contest AI judgments.

## Decision Criteria
Judged by whether the organization's purpose, standards, materials, and verification system are not monopolized by a select few, and whether members can give context to AI, verify AI results, and contest AI judgments.

## Output
The fairness problem of the AI era is redefined not only as diagnosing algorithmic bias, but as a matter of procedural justice—who can give context to AI, who can verify results, and who can contest. It provides the justice-principle grounding for why the context access right (WalkOrder 325) must spread across the whole organization.
