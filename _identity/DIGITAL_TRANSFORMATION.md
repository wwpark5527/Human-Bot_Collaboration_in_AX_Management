# DIGITAL_TRANSFORMATION — DX (Digital Transformation)

## Concept Definition
DX (Digital Transformation) is the transformation paradigm that was popular before AX, focusing on the digitization, datafication, cloudification, and process-efficiency of work and systems. Until the trend shifted to AX in 2026, it was the standard vocabulary of organizational transformation. In the book, DX is used as a preceding concept (foil) structurally contrasted with AX, and it corresponds to layers 1–2 of the LLM system diagram.

## Decision Criteria
If an organization's transformation attempt stays within the five-axis questions of digitization, data storage, process automation, system adoption, and efficiency improvement — "Can this work be digitized?", "How will the data be stored?", "How will the process be automated?", "How will the system be adopted?", "How will efficiency be raised?" — it is judged as DX. Once it begins to address AI's judgment and generation, criteria for data use, human-AI role division, verification and approval, and the distribution of authority, responsibility, and performance, it is judged as going beyond DX into AX.

## Output
Produces the digitization of work and systems, the storage and cloudification of data, the automation of processes, system adoption, and efficiency improvement. It is the standard output form of organizational transformation before AX appeared, and it functions as AX's structural contrast axis and as the output constituting layers 1–2 of the LLM system diagram.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "Whereas DX focused on the digitization, datafication, cloudification, and process-efficiency of work and systems, AX is a transformation that integrates AI into the organization's workflow, judgment system, role structure, deliverable system, authority structure, and responsibility structure."
