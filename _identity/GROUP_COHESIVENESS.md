# GROUP_COHESIVENESS — Group Cohesiveness

## Concept Definition
Group cohesiveness is the established, representative measurement method that academia and industry have used to measure teamwork since before the team-role (TR) concept appeared. It viewed teamwork as the human bond among members — their liking, sticking together, and willingness to help one another, that is, the degree of psychological attraction to cohere and help — and was used as a tool to measure this concretely. It is the structural counterpart axis of the human-bond / similarity-fit side, in contrast to the task-activation / complementary-fit side that team-role balance (TRB) measures.

## Decision Criteria
Distinguished by whether teamwork is viewed as the degree of human bond (the psychological attraction to like, cohere, and help one another) and taken as the object of measurement. This is distinct from taking task activation or the behavioral types exercised within a team (the nine team roles), as TRB does, as the object of measurement.

## Output
The measured value of the degree of human bond (psychological attraction) that academia and industry have produced when measuring teamwork; later, in the "two sides of teamwork" framing proposed by Park Won-woo, it is positioned as the operational measurement method for the human-bond side of teamwork. It becomes the contrast axis showing that the task-activation side must be measured separately by TRB.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "Its concrete measurement mostly made use of group cohesiveness."
