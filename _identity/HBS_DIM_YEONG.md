# HBS_DIM_YEONG — Yeong (靈)

## Concept Definition
Yeong (靈) is the uniquely human dimension, responsible for dignity, morality, responsibility, and subjecthood, in the traditional three-dimensional Yeong-Hon-Baek model. Hon (魂, cognition) and Baek (魄, body) can each be delegated and extended by AI's analysis and reasoning and by robots' physical execution, but Yeong is fundamentally different from Hon and Baek by the criterion of whether a function is outsourceable—Hon and Baek are outsourced, but Yeong cannot be outsourced. The core conclusion of this entire section is that even if AI can extend the human Hon and robots can extend the human Baek, Yeong alone cannot be entrusted to machines.

## Decision Criteria
Whether the function is outsourceable or not—Hon and Baek are outsourced, but Yeong cannot be outsourced.

## Output
It becomes the final decision authority that humans must protect, and losing this place is precisely the honbibaeksan of the AI age. A human who has lost Yeong is not the master, no matter how much technology they hold.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "Yeong (靈)      Dignity, morality, responsibility, subjecthood              The final decision authority that humans must protect"

> "In the end, Yeong in the AI age is the final decision authority. If humans do not give up the last question, technology becomes a tool that extends humans; but if humans give up the last question, technology becomes a system that replaces humans."

> "AI can extend the human Hon. Robots can extend the human Baek. But Yeong cannot be outsourced."

> "A human who has lost Yeong is not the master, no matter how much technology they hold."
