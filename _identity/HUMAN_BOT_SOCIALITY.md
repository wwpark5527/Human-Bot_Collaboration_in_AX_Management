# HUMAN_BOT_SOCIALITY — Human-Bot Sociality

## Concept Definition
Human-bot sociality is an asymmetric trust-and-control relationship: for the human, trust and understandability are central, and the bot recognizes the human as its top-level constraint and reward definer. The nature of the relationship is a master-tool or supervisor-supervisee structure, and its formation rests on trust, understanding, and safety. When a bot's sociality is divided into a human-bot axis and a bot-bot axis, the side designed for trust and control in the relationship with humans is human-bot sociality.

## Decision Criteria
It is judged by whether the relationship is an asymmetric master-tool or supervisor-supervisee structure, and whether explanation, transparency, and verification are required to build trust.

## Output
Demands for explainability, value alignment, and safety, and the building of trust; and an external (social) risk of harm to humans upon failure.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "From the human's standpoint, 'trust + understandability' is central. One comes to ask whether this bot can be trusted, why it made a given judgment, and whether it will harm oneself, and comes to demand explainability, value alignment, and safety."
