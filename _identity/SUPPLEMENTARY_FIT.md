# SUPPLEMENTARY_FIT — Supplementary Fit

## Concept Definition
Supplementary fit is a 20th-century concept of fit that takes members' sense of mutual similarity and homogeneity as one axis of teamwork and uses it to improve departmental and organizational performance. Homogeneity-forming activities such as unity events, "one-heart" rallies, and staff dinners are its representative practices, and this pursuit of similarity and homogeneity does indeed help improve teamwork. However, as the development of transportation and communication and globalization increased exchange and joining with different people, its central position was later ceded to complementary fit, which fills in each other's shortcomings.

## Decision Criteria
Judged by whether it takes similarity and homogeneity among members themselves as the basis for improving organizational performance, and whether that practice focuses on strengthening homogeneity (unity, forming one-heartedness) rather than on respecting and leveraging difference.

## Output
It produces the teamwork advantage of improved departmental and organizational cohesion, but does not produce the aspect of respecting and leveraging diversity (difference), leaving the need to later expand awareness toward respecting diversity and pursuing complementary fit.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "Feeling mutual similarity and homogeneity—this is called supplementary fit—is one aspect of teamwork and does indeed help improve departmental or organizational performance."

> "In the 20th century, there was a strong tendency to see unity through advancing similarity and homogeneity within an organization as the driving force of teamwork and performance improvement. What was emphasized then was supplementary fit."
