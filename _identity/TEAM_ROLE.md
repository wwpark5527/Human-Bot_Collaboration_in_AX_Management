# TEAM_ROLE — Team Role (TR)

## Concept Definition
A team role (TR) is, unlike a functional role that is officially assigned by the organization or superior in relation to the job, an informal role that is not assigned by the organization or superior and has no direct relation to the job (non-job-related), yet is exercised by members in order to form teamwork. It is a concept discovered and named by Belbin (1981). There are many cases where team performance is inadequate even though functional roles are perfectly present within the team; what is additionally needed for teamwork to form is precisely the team role. A team role is not a personality type but a behavior type exercised by a person.

## Decision Criteria
Judged by the fact that, to the question "What is your role?", people present only a functional role—such as "I am in charge of recruiting" or "I am in charge of payroll"—and do not answer "I am the one who makes things fun" or "I am the one who criticizes," which shows that a team role is a separate behavior type distinct from a functional role. In the case of one individual, one may have a functional role that performs the given function well yet fail to correctly perform any one of the several team roles, and conversely one person may perform several team roles well at the same time.

## Output
The team-role concept, as a concept contrasted with the functional role, expanded the concept of "role" and at the same time expanded the concept of "teamwork." By accurately diagnosing and leveraging, per individual, the team roles that are exercised easily and naturally, the latent roles that can be exercised with effort, and the non-preferred roles that cannot be exercised no matter how hard one tries, it leads to a methodology (Team Role Balance, TRB) that dramatically grows team performance.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "A team role is, unlike a functional role that is officially assigned by the organization or superior in relation to the job, an informal role—that is, one not assigned by the organization or superior and with no direct relation to the job (non-job-related)—that is exercised by members in order to form teamwork."
