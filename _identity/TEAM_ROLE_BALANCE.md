# TEAM_ROLE_BALANCE — Team Role Balance (TRB)

## Concept Definition
Team Role Balance (TRB) refers to the state in which all 9 team-role types exist within one team, and moreover exist at the natural-role level. It is a concept named by Belbin: for a team to produce performance, not only the human-bonding aspect of teamwork but also the task-activation aspect of teamwork is needed, and for the latter to form, all 9 team roles must be able to be exercised easily and well within the team. As this book's core methodology, all of Chapters 5 and 6 are structured around this concept.

## Decision Criteria
Judged by whether all 9 team-role types exist within one team, and whether they are exercised at the natural-role level (roles exercised easily and naturally). If they remain at the level of latent/manageable roles or non-preferred roles, TRB is regarded as not established. Regardless of the number of team members, all 9 team roles must exist in the natural-role state.

## Output
The causal chain TRB → Teamwork → Team Performance—that when team role balance is achieved the task-activation aspect of teamwork rises, and when teamwork rises team performance improves—has been proven in numerous empirical studies regardless of country, industry, or organization size. TRB theory not only tells the current level of teamwork and its potential highest possible level, but also provides concrete methods of individual and team management to close that gap and achieve performance.

## Evidence (original quotation)
> "All 9 team-role types must exist within one team, and moreover must exist at the natural-role level. Belbin says that in that case team role balance (TRB) has been achieved."
