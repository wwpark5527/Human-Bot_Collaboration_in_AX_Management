# AI Capability Equality Theory — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- AI capability equality theory (`AI_CAPABILITY_EQUALITY`, S2C-0144) is the concluding theory of the conceptual series running from context capital (WO324) → context access right (WO325) → context justice (WO326), and it stipulates that "inequality in the AI era arises not simply from gaps in access to AI tools but ... from gaps in capability."
- The specific capability pointed to as the cause of inequality is "the capability to convey a human's purpose, standards, and context to AI and to verify, approve, and improve the results" — this corresponds exactly to the ability to handle context capital (purpose, standards, language, materials, format, verification standards, approval standards).
- From this theory the author derives a concrete prescription for organizations: "Therefore an organization must not leave AI-utilization capability to an individual's intuition or to the authority of a particular department. It must convert the context capital, authority system, verification standards, and approval structure that AI can use into a common production base for the whole organization."
- This prescription serves as the conclusion that theoretically justifies the "socialization of context capital" (WalkOrder 323) presented at the opening of this chapter (line 227) — AI capability equality theory is the theoretical ground for why the socialization of context capital is necessary.
- Stage-1 structural role: by clearly distinguishing the AI tool access gap (discussions of the digital-divide kind) from the capability gap (the ability to use context capital), it carries the implication that inequality is not resolved by a policy of simply distributing AI accounts.
