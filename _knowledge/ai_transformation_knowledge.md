# AX (AI Transformation) — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- AX is not a replacement term for DX but an extended concept, and it became necessary as AI moved into the center of organizational operation after DX.
- DX and AX differ in the axis of their questions: DX asks about digitization, data storage, automation, system adoption, and efficiency, while AX asks about AI's judgment and generation, the criteria for data use, the division of roles between humans and AI, verification and approval, and the design of authority, responsibility, and outcome distribution.
- The objective position of AX divides into global expressions (AI Transformation, AI-driven Transformation) and Asian expressions (AX, AI Transformation), and its conceptual origin is "emerging as AI became the center of organizational operation after DX."
- The current mainstream purposes of AX are efficiency, productivity, cost reduction, growth, and work optimization, while inclusive transformation, prevention of AI stratification, benefit sharing, the right of appeal, and the human right of judgment are areas that are still lacking.

## Source Evidence Quotation
> "AX is not a replacement term for DX but an extended concept that became necessary as AI moved into the center of organizational operation after DX. Whereas DX focused on the digitization, datafication, cloud migration, and process efficiency of work and systems, AX is the transformation that integrates AI into the organization's workflows, judgment systems, role structures, output systems, authority structures, and accountability structures."

> "The reason AX is needed is clear. DX alone cannot sufficiently explain the change in which AI intervenes in the organization's judgment, generation, execution, evaluation, and accountability structures. AX is the concept needed to integrate AI into the center of organizational operation and, in that process, to newly design human roles and accountability structures."
