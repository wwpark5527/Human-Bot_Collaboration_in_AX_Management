# Algorithmic Management — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- Algorithmic management (`ALGORITHMIC_MANAGEMENT`, S2C-0137) refers to an algorithmic system that uses tracking data and other information to organize, assign, monitor, supervise, and evaluate work. In this structure, even without a manager judging directly, AI can influence workers' work assignment, performance evaluation, surveillance, discipline, and reward.
- This concept is a core risk that recurs throughout the book, forming the central axis of the AI stratification structure, namely the separation of "humans who use AI" from "humans who are managed by AI." The author states explicitly that "the most dangerous structure in AI stratification is the separation of 'humans who use AI' from 'humans who are managed by AI'."
- The areas to which algorithmic management applies are work assignment, performance evaluation, surveillance, schedule management, reward, discipline, productivity measurement, and the like, and workers are placed in the dual position of being subjects who use AI while at the same time being objects evaluated and managed by AI.
- The author connects algorithmic management directly to the ESG framework and presents five questions: "Who evaluates others with AI? (G: control of authority)", "Who is evaluated by AI? (S: workers' rights)", "Are AI evaluation criteria explained? (G: transparency)", "Can humans object? (S/G: remedy procedure)", "Who is the ultimate responsible party? (G: accountability structure)".
- Algorithmic management reappears as a representative risk factor of efficiency-centered AX (the third item in the list of the limits of efficiency-centered AX: "AI is used for work assignment, performance evaluation, surveillance, and discipline"), and it is positioned as one of the core tasks that inclusive transformation AX must address.
