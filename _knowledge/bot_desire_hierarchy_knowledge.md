# Formation of the bot's desire hierarchy — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- Today's bots do not have desire as humans do; human desire arises from biological and psychological deficiency, but an AI bot has no deficiency and no autonomous desire either.
- AI operates through 'goals, reward functions, and constraints', and this is a matter of optimization rather than of desire.
- Goal-directed behavior and reward-based behavior (reinforcement learning) are presented as the concepts that operate like desire.
- The formation of a desire hierarchy is possible by design: if lower goals (accuracy, speed, safety) are placed under a higher goal (maximizing user satisfaction), the system behaves as if it had a desire hierarchy, and most AI already has this kind of structure.
- A reinforcement-learning-based hierarchy (meta-reward/sub-reward), Hierarchical RL (high-level/low-level policy), and hierarchy in Agentic AI (goal/subgoal) are presented as the concrete ways of implementing it.
- In sum, 'human = internal motivation, AI = external design': an AI bot does not have desire, but if the goal and reward structure is layered, it behaves in a way that looks like desire.
