# Political-policy resistance — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- As the fourth of the 'collective human reaction/resistance types (5 types),' it is the stage following sociocultural resistance (the cultural and discursive dimension), and is resistance that seeks to control AI through the formal institutions of legislation and regulation.
- Its core features are strengthening AI regulation, emphasizing data sovereignty, restricting AI use, and securing human control.
- The U.S. case is the 'debates over AI regulation laws' among parts of Congress, which are pushing AI transparency obligations, copyright regulation, AI safety verification, and deepfake regulation, with the background being the concern that "AI may infringe on democracy, labor, and human rights."
- The European case is the EU AI Act, whose core elements are restrictions on high-risk AI, human-oversight obligations, explainability requirements, and limits on biometric surveillance, and this becomes the foundation of the 'human-centered AI' philosophy.
