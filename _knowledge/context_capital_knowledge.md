# Context Capital — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- Context capital (`CONTEXT_CAPITAL`, S2C-0141) is the accumulated operating asset of the purpose, (judgment) standards, language, materials, format, verification standards, and approval standards needed to make AI work properly. As one of the core new concepts this book presents, it is defined first in this chapter ("Related core concepts").
- The author states that "In the AI era, money, data, models, and compute are not the only capital. The organizational context that can be given to AI is also capital," thereby adding context capital to the traditional concept of capital (financial, data, model, and compute capital).
- The sentence "The power to direct AI comes not from mere prompt technique but from the ability to design what purpose to give, by what standards to have it judge, what materials to have it use, and what results to approve" stipulates the practical implication of context capital — it emphasizes that the essence of the ability to use AI is not technical proficiency but the ability to design context.
- Context capital immediately leads on to a new axis of stratification, the "context access right" — "Stratification in the AI era arises not only from access to models but from the context access right. When an organization's context capital is monopolized by a few, the ability to use AI becomes subordinate to an individual's sense, language ability, information access, rank, and departmental authority."
- Context capital, as a higher-level concept, is made concrete at the working level as the "common context": "Context capital is the production base that sets out, within a particular task, the purpose, standards, roles, sources, and formats according to which AI is to work. The common context is a local operating concept that structures this context capital so that AI can use it at the actual work unit."
