# Context Justice — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- Context Justice (`CONTEXT_JUSTICE`, S2C-0143) is the "related core concept" that follows immediately after the context capital access right (WalkOrder 325), and is presented as the principle of justice that must be applied when AI participates in important judgments and outputs.
- The author states explicitly that this concept fundamentally reconstructs the discussion of fairness in the AI era: "It does not view fairness in the AI era merely as a matter of algorithmic bias."
- The three core questions of the reconstructed fairness are stated: "More fundamentally, it views it as the questions of who can give context to AI, who can verify AI results, and who can contest AI judgments." These three questions (the right to give context / the right to verify / the right of appeal) become the theoretical foundation for the concrete list of rights, such as the "AI right of judgment," the "AI right of explanation," and the "AI right of appeal," presented later in the extension of S (ESG_EXT_S_AXIS, WalkOrder 329).
- Context Justice defines the monopolization of "the organization's purpose, standards, materials, and verification system" by a select few as injustice (不正義), and as the alternative presents access to "the common production base on which members can work together with AI" — it is context capital (WO324) and the context capital access right (WO325) elevated to the level of a principle of justice.
- Stage-1 structural role: as a theory of justice centered on procedure and access rights, contrasted with the existing discussion of AI fairness centered on algorithmic bias, it leads directly to the next concept, the theory of AI capability equality (WalkOrder 327).
