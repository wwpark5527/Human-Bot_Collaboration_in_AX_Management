# Efficiency-centered AX — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- Efficiency-centered AX (`EFFICIENCY_CENTERED_AX`, S2C-0139) is an AX approach that can raise a company's productivity and competitiveness, but when it is designed solely on the criterion of efficiency, six risks arise: reduction of human roles, widening of AI-competence gaps, algorithmic management (abuse), weakening of judgment authority, absence of the right to object, and performance concentration.
- This concept functions as the explicit foil of inclusive-transformation AX. The author compares the two approaches in a table: core aim (productivity, cost reduction, speed vs. productivity + protection of human competence + accountability), the position of the human (AI user or object of replacement vs. subject who judges, verifies, and improves together with AI), organizational concern (automation, optimization, growth vs. access rights, learning rights, judgment rights, the right to object, benefit sharing), performance criteria (ROI, cost reduction, increased workload vs. ROI + competence distribution + accountability + fairness), principal risk (deepening of AI stratification vs. prevention and mitigation of AI stratification), and connection to ESG (limited vs. the core execution structure of S and G).
- The author summarizes that "efficiency-centered AX creates 'a more efficient organization through AI'," and states that inclusive-transformation AX does not deny this but demands redesign "because efficiency alone is not sufficient."
- The "algorithmic management" risk appearing in efficiency-centered AX connects directly to the concept of algorithmic management defined separately earlier, and reappears as the concrete risk that "AI is used for work assignment, performance evaluation, surveillance, and discipline."
