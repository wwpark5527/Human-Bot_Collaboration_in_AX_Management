# Extension of E (Sustainability of AI Infrastructure) — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- The extension of E (`ESG_EXT_E_AXIS`, S2C-0484) is the first of the three axes of the extension of the ESG concept in the AI era (S2C-0146, ESG_EXTENSION), and it starts from the problem awareness that "in corporate operations in the AI era, the meaning of the environment is not sufficiently covered by existing industrial facilities or physical production facilities alone."
- Statistics from international organizations are cited as the grounds for the extension: "The IEA (2025) forecasts that global data-center electricity consumption may reach about 945 TWh by 2030. UNEP (2025) explains that AI data centers are connected to problems of electricity, water use, e-waste, rare minerals, and greenhouse-gas emissions, and the OECD (2022) likewise analyzes that the environmental impact of AI compute and applications must be measured."
- The six management items of the E axis and their organizational and social implications are presented in a table: electricity use (problems of the power grid and energy costs), water use (conflicts over local communities and water resources), carbon emissions (whether renewable energy is used), semiconductors and minerals (competition over resource supply chains), e-waste (problems of equipment replacement and waste), and local environmental impact (the possibility of conflict with local communities due to data-center siting, power grids, cooling water, and land use).
- The comparison table with the existing ESG (lines 324-334) shows that the existing E of "environment, energy, carbon, resources, pollution" is explicitly extended in the extended ESG to "the environmental responsibility of AI infrastructure, data centers, compute, cloud, and digital operations."
- Concluding sentence: "AI thus operates on top of large-scale physical infrastructure such as large-scale electricity, water, semiconductors, and data centers. Therefore E is no longer simply a matter of factories, facilities, and carbon emissions, but must be extended in the direction of including the impact that the entire corporate infrastructure of the AI era has on the environment, and the environmental impact of AI must be managed on the E axis of ESG."
- Stage-1/2 structure: originally it was the single candidate S1C-171 "extension of the ESG concept in the AI era (extended ESG)," but in Stage-2 it was SPLIT into the three axes E/S/G (parent S2C-0146). This knowledge file corresponds to the E-axis fragment among them (S2C-0484).
