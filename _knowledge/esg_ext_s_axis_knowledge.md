# Extension of S (AI Capability Equality and Human Dignity) — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- The extension of S (`ESG_EXT_S_AXIS`, S2C-0485) is the second of the three axes of the extension of the ESG concept in the AI era (S2C-0146, ESG_EXTENSION), and it starts from the problem awareness that "AI directly affects labor and social structure. In particular, AI in which a world model and a knowledge chain are combined can affect workers' roles, capabilities, evaluation, placement, education, employment stability, and the human right of judgment."
- Seven questions that the S axis of the AI era must include are presented in a table: how AI changes workers' roles (replacement / complementation / redeployment / upskilling), whether humans can understand and appeal AI outcomes (explainability / procedural fairness), whether AI is used for evaluation and placement (algorithmic management / workers' rights), whether the capability to use AI is provided equally (education / retraining / accessibility), whether AI reinforces discrimination and bias (data bias), whether the human right of judgment is maintained (human oversight / ultimate responsibility), and whether context design is in place (sharing of purpose, criteria, and verification).
- The core proposition: "In an era when the interaction of humans and AI becomes the core of corporate operation, understanding corporate social responsibility only as a human-to-human relationship is insufficient. Therefore, because social risk in the AI era is not only a question of 'whether AI replaces people', S must address the social quality of an organization in which humans and AI work together."
- The definition of the S-axis responsibility in the AI era: "S in the AI era is the responsibility to guarantee (by granting the following rights) the social capabilities enabling humans to understand, use, and control AI, to raise objections, and to participate in its outcomes, rather than being beings replaced, surveilled, and excluded by AI."
- Eight concrete rights are enumerated: the AI access right (access to the AI tools and infrastructure needed for work), the AI right to learn (literacy, reskilling, upskilling), the AI right to use (the right to use AI for capability expansion), the AI right of judgment (the right to provide purpose, meaning, and criteria), the AI right to explanation (the right to understand outcomes and evaluation criteria), the AI right of appeal (the right to demand re-review), the AI right to transition (the right to receive role redesign and transition education), and the AI right to benefit sharing (a compensation structure for productivity improvement).
- The comparison table with existing ESG (lines 328-330) shows that the existing S of "labor, human rights, safety, diversity, social contribution" is explicitly extended in extended ESG into "AI access rights, the right to learn, role transition, the human right of judgment, equality in the capability to use AI."
- Stage-1/2 structure: it was originally the single candidate S1C-171 "extension of the ESG concept in the AI era (extended ESG)," but in Stage-2 it was SPLIT into the three axes E/S/G (parent S2C-0146). This knowledge file corresponds to the S-axis fragment among them (S2C-0485), and it is the version that concretizes into eight rights the preceding context justice (WO326) and theory of AI capability equality (WO327).
