# Measurement-verification-strengthening theory — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- The measurement-verification-strengthening theory (`ESGX_MEASUREMENT_VERIFICATION_CRITIQUE`, S2C-0488) is the second of the three critique types classified by ESG extension theory (S2C-0147, `ESG_EXTENSION_THEORY`). Whereas the stakeholder-expansion theory (WalkOrder 331) takes issue with the object of ESG (for whom is ESG), this critique takes issue with the measurement and verification reliability of ESG (how ESG is measured).
- The core of the critique is greenwashing: "This criticizes the prevalence of disguised environmentalism such as greenwashing, saying that without unified criteria scores varied from one rating agency to another, and that companies abused it as a means to disclose only favorable data and package themselves as eco-friendly." That is, the problem has two branches: (1) score discrepancies caused by the non-unification of criteria across rating agencies, and (2) distortion caused by companies' selective disclosure of data.
- The output this critique demands is unified evaluation and disclosure criteria and the strengthening of the verification system — that is, it demands institutional devices that guarantee the reliability and comparability of ESG scores themselves.
