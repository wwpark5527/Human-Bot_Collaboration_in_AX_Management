# Stakeholder-expansion theory — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- The stakeholder-expansion theory (`ESGX_STAKEHOLDER_EXPANSION_CRITIQUE`, S2C-0487) is the first of the three critique types classified by ESG extension theory (S2C-0147, `ESG_EXTENSION_THEORY`). ESG extension theory itself is introduced as "not yet a single unified theory, but a current in which international organizations, investment institutions, and academia point out the limitations of the existing ESG from various angles and seek to reconstruct it."
- The core of the critique: "The criticism is that the existing ESG only considered how risky climate change or human-rights issues were to a company's financial condition (its money-making) rather than the issues themselves." That is, the thesis of this critique is that ESG calculates only the risk that returns to company finances (single materiality) rather than the impact on the planet and society itself.
- The representative actor raising this critique is international organizations, especially the EU: the EU seeks "to break down head-on the 'company-profit-centered viewpoint'," and through the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), which has been implemented in stages since 2024, it "is forcibly shifting the concept of ESG from financial materiality (the existing ESG concept) to impact materiality." This is summarized as "changing the structure so that one cannot be said to do ESG well merely by making money well."
- The output this critique type demands is the shift from financial materiality to impact materiality, and the EU CSRD is presented as its concrete institutional case.
- Stage-1/2 structure: the original candidate S1C-172 "ESG extension theory" was SPLIT in Stage-2 into three critique types (stakeholder-expansion theory/measurement-verification-strengthening theory/ESG AI-era extension theory) (parent S2C-0147). This knowledge file is the first of those fragments (S2C-0487), the first of the three siblings spanning WalkOrder 331-333.
