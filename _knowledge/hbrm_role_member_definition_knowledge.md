# Member Definition — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- HBRM is a management system that, in an AX organization where humans and bots become members together, accepts humans and bots as the core resources that create the organization's performance and learning, has them developed, deployed, and collaborating, and designs them to evolve together under trust and governance.
- Whereas existing HRM was human resource management centered on human members, HBRM extends the object of management from humans to humans and bots.
- Including bots does not mean regarding bots as persons or as accountable subjects identical to humans; it means that, because bots actually perform work, collaborate with humans, and affect the organization's performance and risk, they must be managed within the role, authority, verification, and responsibility structure.
- HBRM performs eight roles in an AX organization (member definition, role design, collaboration-structure design, augmentation management, role-balance management, trust management, governance linkage, learning and improvement management), and "member definition" is the first of these, the role that defines the positions and roles of H, AH, B, and AB.
- HBRM is neither mere personnel management nor technology operation; it is an integrated management system that deals together with the relationships, roles, collaboration, responsibility, and growth of humans and bots.
