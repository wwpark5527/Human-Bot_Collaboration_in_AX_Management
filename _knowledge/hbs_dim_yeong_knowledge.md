# Yeong (靈) — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- In the traditional yeong-hon-baek perspective, yeong has dignity, morality, responsibility, and agency as its core meaning, and in the age of AI and robots it corresponds to the final right of judgment that humans must protect.
- Hon (魂) and baek (魄) can be outsourced to AI and robots, but yeong cannot be outsourced, and this is both the criterion and the conclusion of this entire section.
- Yeong in the AI age does not remain a religious concept; it is the concrete values that humans must protect in order to remain subjects: the ability to judge for oneself, the attitude of taking responsibility for one's own actions, the sense that understands others' suffering, the value that puts life and dignity first, the strength to ask about meaning rather than efficiency, and the agency to refuse the system's commands.
- AI can produce answers, but whether to accept, reject, revise, or take responsibility for those answers is the human's part, and robots can carry out actions, but whether those actions are right, whom they are for, and what harm they create must be judged by humans — this is yeong as the final right of judgment.
- A human who has lost yeong is not the master, no matter how much technology they have, and a human who has protected yeong can still remain a subject even while using AI and robots.
