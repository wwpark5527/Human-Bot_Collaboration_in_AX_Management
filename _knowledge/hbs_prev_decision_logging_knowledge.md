# AI's decision-making process must be recorded — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- As the second of the five practice guidelines in the section on honbibaeksan prevention measures, the four items that must be recorded are the grounds of judgment, the data used, the approver, and the risks reviewed.
- The core logic of this guideline is that "if there is no record, responsibility disappears as well" — responsibility is not maintained by abstract declaration alone, and it persists in substance only when it is backed by traceable records.
- This guideline is the evidence-based device that keeps the first guideline (ultimate responsibility must remain with humans and the organization) from stopping at the declarative level and makes it actually verifiable — even if the subject of responsibility is designated, that responsibility cannot be confirmed or pursued after the fact if there is no record of the judgment process.
- What is recorded is not only the "content" of AI's judgment but the entire "process" by which the judgment was made (grounds, data, approver, risk review), and this also connects to the later guidelines (the human's role as reviewer, governance design).

## Source Evidence Quotation
> "AI's judgment process must be recorded: on what grounds AI judged, what data was used, who approved it, and what risks were reviewed must be recorded. If there is no record, responsibility disappears as well."
