# Technology adoption must be designed together with governance — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- It is the last of the five practical guidelines in the 'honbibaeksan-prevention measures' section, a structural concluding guideline that encompasses the preceding four guidelines (the attribution of final responsibility to the human, recording the decision process, the human's reviewer role, and preventing componentization) — it is not a principle for individual judgment and action situations but a design principle for the adoption stage itself.
- The core of the problem is identified as the fact that, when an organization adopts AI, it answers only the functional question of "what can AI do" and skips the permitted-scope question of "how far may AI go".
- Governance is made concrete as the six elements of authority, responsibility, approval, recording, auditing, and risk review, and this corresponds to a higher-level frame that integrates the requirements of the preceding four guidelines (attribution of responsibility, recording, the reviewer role) at the adoption design stage.
- If this guideline is not observed, it can lead to the risk that function-centered adoption comes first and governance is designed retroactively afterwards or omitted altogether, that is, to honbibaeksan.

## Source Evidence Quotation
> "Technology adoption must be designed together with governance: when an organization adopts AI, it must not look only at function. More important than what AI can do is determining how far AI may go. Therefore, AI adoption must be designed together with governance, including authority, responsibility, approval, recording, auditing, and risk review."
