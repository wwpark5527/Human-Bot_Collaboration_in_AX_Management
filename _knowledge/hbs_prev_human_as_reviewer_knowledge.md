# Humans must be reviewers of AI output, not consumers — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- It is the third of the five practical guidelines in the 'honbibaeksan-prevention measures' section, and it requires that the human attitude toward AI output be converted from 'consumption' to 'review'.
- The required behaviors are three: interpret, verify, and adjust — accepting output as it is without doing all three is the 'consumer' attitude.
- This guideline redefines the human role in the AI age — not a mere user but a judge and verifier.
- This guideline connects directly to the preceding concept of 'honbi' (the loss of the sovereignty of judgment) — honbi was the state in which the human does not question for themselves and accepts the answer presented by AI as it is, and this guideline codifies the opposite behavior (interpretation, verification, adjustment) as an organization-level obligation.

## Source Evidence Quotation
> "Humans must be reviewers of AI output, not consumers: rather than accepting AI-produced results as they are, they must be interpreted, verified, and adjusted. The human role in the AI age is not a mere user but a judge and verifier."

> "Honbi (the loss of the sovereignty of judgment): honbi is the state in which the human Hon, that is, the center of thought and judgment, passes to AI. The human does not question for themselves and accepts the answer presented by AI as it is."
