# Automation must not reduce humans to components — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- It is the fourth of the five practical guidelines in the 'honbibaeksan-prevention measures' section, and while it does not deny the efficiency improvement of robots and automation systems as such, it specifies the four objects of protection (rest, safety, dignity, right of judgment) that must be upheld in that process.
- This guideline connects directly to the concept of 'baeksan' (the systematization of body and labor) — baeksan was the state in which the human body and labor lose their own purpose and scatter within platform labor, surveillance systems, production algorithms, and data-based deployment systems.
- The core risk of baeksan is that, although on the surface the human appears to move freely, in reality they come to move according to the rhythms and rules the system determines, and this guideline places constraints from the automation design stage itself so that such a state does not follow.
- Efficiency (the extension of Baek) and human protection (the prevention of baeksan) can be conflicting goals, and this guideline codifies the principle that human protection must not be sacrificed for reasons of efficiency.

## Source Evidence Quotation
> "Automation must not reduce humans to components: robots and automation systems can raise efficiency. But in that process, human rest, safety, dignity, and decision authority must not be infringed."

> "Baeksan (the systematization of body and labor): baeksan is the state in which the human body and labor lose their own purpose and scatter within automation systems. Platform labor, surveillance systems, production algorithms, and data-based deployment systems organize (form) human behavior efficiently. On the surface the human appears to move freely, but in reality they come to move according to the rhythms and rules the system determines."
