# Accountability Structure — Knowledge

To measure this indicator correctly, one must know exactly **what the source text demanded and did not demand** of this item. The source text is only one line, and what that one line demands is a single designation rate.

## Core Knowledge

- **The value the source text demands is one ratio.** "Rate of designating a final responsible party for AI outputs" — the numerator is outputs for which a final responsible party is designated, the denominator is all AI outputs. The source text does not prescribe the content, scope, or level of responsibility. What this indicator measures is not the quality of responsibility but the **presence or absence of designation**, and the measurer must not add requirements not prescribed here.
- **The qualifier "final" is the core of the measurement.** The source text speaks not of a responsible party but of the **final** responsible party. When several parties are involved in one output, having a list of involved parties and having one final attributee fixed are different things. A case with only a list of involved parties is not counted as a designation. A case in which multiple final responsible parties are recorded is also classified and reported separately, since it cannot identify a respondent in the event of an accident.
- **Department-level designation is indistinguishable from non-designation.** This is not a requirement stated in the source text but a measurement discipline that must be followed if the designation rate is to have meaning. For an output on which "the AI Innovation Team" is recorded as the final responsible party, when a problem arises no one within the team recognizes that it is their turn to answer. For the designation rate to be a value that measures the accountability gap, the numerator must be at the level of an **individual identifier**.
- **Retroactive after-the-fact designation turns the designation rate into an after-the-fact blame rate.** If a responsible party is attached after an output has already been used, the figure rises but no structure comes into being. An accountability structure means a state in which who will answer is fixed before an output is made, so a designation rate submitted without a prior-designation ratio alongside it cannot be interpreted.
- **The average conceals departmental gaps.** Because this indicator summarizes the whole organization in one value, it cannot distinguish whether a designation rate of 90% is 90% evenly across all departments or 100% in nine departments and 0% in one. In the latter case that one department has no accountability structure at all. This is why the per-department minimum must be reported alongside.
- **This indicator depends on audit records for its actual measurement.** The fact of designation and the time of designation can be counted only if records remain. If the retention rate of the approval and modification records demanded by the preceding indicator `IND_AUDIT_RECORD` (WalkOrder 366, source line 543) is low, the numerator of this indicator shrinks from the actual number of designations to the **number of designations left in the records**. Therefore, when the designation rate comes out low, one must first distinguish whether there was no designation or no record, and a designation rate produced without this distinction points at the wrong target for improvement.
