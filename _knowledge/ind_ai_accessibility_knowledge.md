# AI Accessibility — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- "AI accessibility" (`IND_AI_ACCESSIBILITY`, S2C-0513) is the **first indicator** constituting the "12 Inclusive Transition ESG indicators" (`INCLUSIVE_TRANSITION_ESG_12_INDICATORS`, S2C-0158, class INDEX). Definition: "an indicator that looks at the degree to which employees can actually access AI tools and the variation in access between departments." The criteria are "employee AI-tool access rate and inter-departmental access gap," and the output is "the access-rate figure and the inter-departmental gap value."
- The higher context of the 12 indicators as a whole is source line 531 — "Furthermore, AI inclusive-transition ESG must be measurable. The following indicators (provisionally called the 12 Inclusive Transition ESG indicators) are basic items for confirming whether an organization operates its AI transition responsibly." That is, the 12 indicators are the layer that deals with 'how it is confirmed that operation is done properly' following the preceding 9-step execution model, which dealt with 'how it is operated'. This indicator is placed at line 533 as the first item of that list.
- **This indicator looks at two values together** — the access "rate" and the access "gap". The grounds for this are that the source text wrote "employee AI-tool access rate" and "inter-departmental access gap" side by side within one item. It is a design in which one aggregate indicator alone is insufficient. Even if the company-wide average access rate is high, if a particular department is excluded it is a failure from the standpoint of inclusive transition, so the indicator functions properly only when the total (rate) and the distribution (gap) are measured simultaneously.
- What this indicator measures corresponds to the first right in the rights layer. The first item of the 8 rights is "right of AI access: the opportunity to access the AI tools and infrastructure required for work" (line 487), and this indicator confirms in figures how far that right is actually guaranteed. The right prescribes what must be guaranteed (`RIGHT_AI_ACCESS`, WalkOrder 342), and the indicator counts how far it has been realized (this identity). The two nodes belong to different layers.
- The problem this indicator seeks to expose is itself already conceptualized in the preceding section. The first gap item in the discussion of AI-based stratification is `GAP_AI_ACCESS` (AI access gap, WalkOrder 309), and the "inter-departmental access gap" value of this indicator becomes the tool for measuring how far that gap has actually widened within the organization. That is, the relation **gap (problem concept) -> right (guarantee norm) -> indicator (measurement tool)** holds.
- In the Stage-1 structure, S1C-183 was classified as class **INDEX** (this classification was transferred as is from the Stage-1 C0 row), and in Stage-2 it was SPLIT and divided into individual indicator fragments. This candidate is the **first fragment** of that, and with this closure the 12-indicator series begins on the roster.
- **Note the placement of the source list.** The list of 12 indicators is not continuous. The indicators continue at lines 533-539, then at line 541 a paragraph concerning the EU AI Act, UNESCO, and the OECD is inserted, and the remaining indicators resume at lines 543-548. This identity (line 533) and the next item (AI education, line 534) lie before that interruption point and are unaffected, but when quoting the latter part of the list the line numbers must be confirmed individually.
