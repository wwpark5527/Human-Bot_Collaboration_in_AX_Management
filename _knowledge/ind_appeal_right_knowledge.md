# Right to Appeal — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- "Right to appeal" (`IND_APPEAL_RIGHT`, S2C-0519) is the **seventh indicator** constituting the "12 Inclusive Transition ESG indicators" (`INCLUSIVE_TRANSITION_ESG_12_INDICATORS`, S2C-0158, class INDEX). Definition: "an indicator that looks at whether requests to review AI results are actually received and processed." The criteria are "number of requests to review AI results and correction/remedy processing rate," and the output is "number of review requests and correction/remedy processing rate."
- The higher context of the 12 indicators as a whole is source line 531 — "Furthermore, AI inclusive-transition ESG must be measurable. The following indicators (provisionally called the 12 Inclusive Transition ESG indicators) are basic items for confirming whether an organization operates its AI transition responsibly." This indicator is placed at line 539 as the seventh item of that list, and it is **the last item of the stretch in which the indicators run continuously in the source (lines 533-539)**.
- **The two values of this indicator are inflow and outcome.** The "number of review requests" is the inflow-side value measuring whether objections were actually received, and the "correction/remedy processing rate" is the outcome-side value measuring whether those objections changed the result. Even if the channel is open, if it does not lead to processing then appeal is a formality, and even if the processing rate is high, if requests are not received in the first place then the channel is closed.
- **The number of requests is a value whose direction is not fixed.** This is the point requiring the most caution in this indicator. A large number may mean that there are many problems with the AI decisions, or it may mean that the channel for raising objections is well open. Conversely, a small number may mean that the decisions were accurate, or it may mean that people did not know the channel or considered it useless to raise anything. Therefore **a request count of 0 is not in itself a good value**, and cannot be interpreted before the existence and accessibility of the channel are confirmed. Unlike the values of the preceding indicators, whose direction was generally fixed, this value has meaning only when the grounds for its interpretation are recorded alongside it.
- **The numerator of the processing rate must be set as "correction/remedy," not "closure."** The source text wrote not "processing rate" but "**correction/remedy** processing rate." Even if all received requests are reviewed and dismissed, the closure rate becomes 100%, but that is not the value this indicator demands. The numerator is the cases in which the decision was actually corrected or the party concerned was remedied, and it does not include dismissals or mere closures. In addition, the time taken to process must be recorded alongside — because even if a result comes out, if it is excessively late it cannot function as a remedy.
- **The preceding indicator is a precondition of this indicator.** In a state where the user understanding of explainability (line 538, `IND_EXPLAINABILITY`, WalkOrder 364) is low, it is natural for the number of review requests to come out low. If the grounds for a decision are not understood, one cannot establish on what grounds to raise an objection. Therefore the number of requests in this indicator must always be read together with the understanding level of the preceding indicator, and if understanding is low and the number of requests is also low, that is not grounds for saying the decisions were accurate but is closer to a signal that objections could not be established.
- **It must be distinguished from the execution-step layer.** The sixth step of the 9-step ESG execution structure is "6. Appeal procedure" (line 525, `STEP_APPEAL_PROCEDURE`, WalkOrder 355), and its execution content is "putting in place a structure for explanation, review, and correction requests concerning AI results". That is, **putting in place** the appeal channel is step 6, and **counting** whether requests actually came in through that channel and led to correction or remedy is this indicator. This identity does not take "puts a structure in place" as its own procedure; it measures only the results of the structure that has been put in place having operated.
- **It must also be distinguished from the rights layer.** The sixth item of the 8 rights is "right of AI appeal: the right to demand review of AI assessments, recommendations, and decisions" (line 492, `RIGHT_AI_APPEAL`, WalkOrder 347). The indicator's name is "right to appeal" and the right's name is "right of AI appeal," differing only by one prefix. But the layers differ — the right prescribes the entitlement to demand review, and this indicator counts whether that demand was actually received and processed. **This node must not be read as a right merely because the indicator's name contains "right"**; what is measured is the operational fact of the number of requests and the correction/remedy processing rate. The same caution applies to the preceding human right of judgment indicator (`IND_HUMAN_JUDGMENT_RIGHT`, WalkOrder 363).
- In the Stage-1 structure, S1C-183 was classified as class **INDEX** (a value transferred as is from the Stage-1 C0 row), and in Stage-2 it was SPLIT and divided into individual indicator fragments. This candidate is the **seventh fragment** of that.
- **Note the placement of the source list — the list breaks off immediately after this item.** The list of 12 indicators is not continuous. The indicators run at lines 533-539, and this identity (line 539) is the last of that stretch; the immediately following line 540 is a blank line, and **line 541 is a paragraph concerning the EU AI Act, UNESCO, and the OECD**, content unrelated to the indicators. The remaining indicators resume from line 543. Therefore, when looking for the indicator following this item, one must not skim lines 540-542, and it must be confirmed individually that the line number of the next item (audit record) is 543.
