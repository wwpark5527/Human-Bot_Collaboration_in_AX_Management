## Core Knowledge
- The AI-displacement/reduction-risk worker (`LABOR_AI_DISPLACEMENT_RISK_WORKER`, S2C-0475) is the third of the four labor-differentiation types of the AI era: a worker type placed at risk of having their job reduced or replaced by the automation of repetitive, documented work.
- The criterion is "whether the assigned work is repetitive, documented work that is a target for automation," and the change in the organization/labor field is that "the value of the work may fall or disappear."
- As background to the change in the labor market, the author cites the IMF (2024) analysis that "about 60% of jobs in advanced economies may be affected by AI" and the ILO (2025) analysis that "generative AI is more likely to transform jobs at the task level than to replace entire occupations at once," which suggests that the risk of this type is closer to "a gradual reduction in the value of tasks and work" than to "the immediate disappearance of whole jobs."
- The author emphasizes that "the core is not only the number of jobs; what matters more is how the worker's role, authority, mode of evaluation, and bargaining power change," making clear that the essential risk of this type lies not in simple unemployment statistics but in the erosion of role and bargaining power.
- Positioned third among the four types, it stands in a relatively passive and vulnerable position in contrast to the next and final type, the context-designer AX talent (the role that actively designs purposes, standards, verification, and feedback).
