# ② Obedience to Humans (Recognition of Dignity and Hierarchy) — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- The three laws of robotics are principles presented by the science fiction writer I. Asimov in the short story Runaround (1942), and they are the most classical and representative content imposing the duty of respect for humans and safety on robots.
- The Second Law (obedience to humans) is a conditional duty stating that a robot must obey human orders as long as they do not conflict with the First Law (respect for human safety and life).
- Obedience is not an absolute duty but a subordinate principle under the First Law, and the Third Law (self-preservation) likewise holds only within the range that does not conflict with the First and Second Laws.
- Teaching respect for humans to a bot is not a matter of education but a matter of designing the system so that only actions that do not infringe upon humans are selected, and obedience too must be designed as part of this action, reward, and verification structure.

## Source Evidence Quotation
