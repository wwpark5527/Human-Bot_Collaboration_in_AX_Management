# ③ Robot's own self-preservation (maintenance of existence) — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- The three laws of robotics are principles presented by the science fiction writer I. Asimov in the short story Runaround (1942), and they are the most classical and representative content imposing the duty of respect for humans and safety on robots.
- The Third Law (self-preservation) is the lowest-ranking conditional duty, stating that a robot must protect itself as long as doing so does not conflict with the First Law (respect for human safety and life) and the Second Law (obedience to humans).
- Self-preservation is a principle subordinate to the two higher principles, and human safety, life, and orders take precedence over the maintenance of the bot's own existence.
- Teaching respect for humans to a bot is not a matter of education but a matter of designing the system so that only actions that do not infringe upon humans are selected, and self-preservation too must be designed with the lowest priority within this action, reward, and verification structure.
