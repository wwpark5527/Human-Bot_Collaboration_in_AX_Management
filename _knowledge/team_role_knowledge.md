# Team Role (TR) — Knowledge

## Core Knowledge
- A team role (TR) is, unlike a functional role that is officially assigned by the organization or superior in relation to the job, an informal role that has no direct relation to the job (non-job-related) yet is exercised by members in order to form teamwork. Belbin (1981) discovered it and gave it its name.
- There are many cases where team performance is inadequate even though functional roles are perfectly present within the team; for teamwork to form, a team role is additionally needed.
- A team role is not a personality type but a behavior type exercised by a person — personality influences behavior to a considerable extent, but adults do not act solely according to their personality, and actual behavior can vary with learned experience and effort.
- Example: asked "What is your role?", people answer "I am in charge of recruiting" (functional role), not "I am the one who makes things fun" (team role). One individual may perform the functional role well yet fail to correctly perform any one of the team roles, and conversely may perform several team roles well at the same time.
- The team role, contrasted with the functional role, expanded the concept of "role" and at the same time expanded the concept of "teamwork" — the atomic unit that team role balance (TRB) balances is precisely the team role.
