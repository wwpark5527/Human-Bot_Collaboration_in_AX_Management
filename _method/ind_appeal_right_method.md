# Right to Appeal — Method

## Method / Procedure
1. Identify the intake routes for review requests — confirm which of the official desk, direct submission to the person in charge, and oral submission are left as records, and if there is a route that is not recorded, state that fact explicitly.
2. Fix the recognized scope of requests — prescribe for which AI results (assessments, recommendations, allocations, screenings, and so on) objections will be counted.
3. Tally the **number of requests to review AI results** — record them divided by decision type and by department, and mark separately the ranges in which there was not a single request.
4. Secure the grounds for interpreting the number of requests alongside — record together whether an appeal channel exists, whether the persons concerned know of and can access that channel, and at what level the user understanding of the explainability indicator stands. Do not present the number alone without these grounds.
5. Classify the processing results — divide them into (a) decision corrected, (b) party concerned remedied, (c) dismissed, and (d) unprocessed or delayed.
6. Calculate the **correction/remedy processing rate** — the numerator is the sum of (a) and (b), and dismissals and mere closures are not included in the numerator. The denominator is the total number of requests received.
7. Record the time taken to process — present alongside the period from receipt to notification of the result, so that a late remedy is not counted as a remedy.
8. Present the number of requests and the correction/remedy processing rate bound together as one reporting unit.
9. Fix a measurement cycle and accumulate a time series on the same standard. Since an increase or decrease in the number of requests does not in itself mean improvement or deterioration, the trend must always be read together with the interpretive grounds of 4.

## Criteria
With the intake routes and the recognized scope of requests stated explicitly, it is PASS when **both the number of review requests and the correction/remedy processing rate** are calculated, the numerator of the processing rate is limited to **cases that led to correction or remedy**, the grounds for interpretation (channel accessibility and understanding of explanations) are attached to the number of requests, and a time series on the same standard is accumulated. It is judged FAIL if any one of the following applies — (a) the case in which dismissals or mere closures were included in the numerator so that the processing rate was calculated as a closure rate, (b) the case in which a request count of 0 or a low count was reported as a good state without grounds for interpretation, (c) the case in which only the number of requests was tallied and the correction/remedy processing rate was not produced. (a) makes 100% come out even when everything is dismissed after review, (b) turns a state in which the channel is closed into a state in which there are no objections, and (c) fails to confirm whether the objections received changed the result, and each therefore runs counter to the purpose of this indicator.
