# Explainability — Method

## Method / Procedure
1. Fix the scope of the AI decisions subject to explanation — state explicitly to which decision types (assessment, recommendation, allocation, screening, and so on) the duty of explanation attaches. Since this scope is the denominator of the provision rate, fix it throughout the measurement cycle and, if it is changed, report the fact of the change alongside.
2. Fix the minimum requirements of an explanation — prescribe which of the grounds of the decision, the data used, the criteria applied, and the route of appeal are to be regarded as mandatory. A notification that does not meet the requirements is not tallied as an explanation provided.
3. Calculate the **AI-decision explanation-provision rate** — compute the proportion of cases in which an explanation meeting the minimum requirements was delivered, relative to the decisions subject to explanation.
4. Fix the method of measuring comprehension — do not use satisfaction questions. Use a restatement method having people restate the grounds of the decision, or a confirmation-question method asking about the criteria applied, and attach the questions themselves to the output.
5. Measure **user comprehension** — apply the method of 4 to the people who received explanations, and record alongside the response rate and the composition of the sample (job, grade, experience of using AI). If the response rate is low, state that fact explicitly together with the figures.
6. Decompose comprehension by group — confirm whether comprehension comes out low in particular jobs or grades. This is the step for finding a state in which the form of explanation works only for a particular group.
7. Present the provision rate and comprehension bound together as one reporting unit, and mark as a separate item the **ranges in which the provision rate is high but comprehension is low**.
8. Fix a measurement cycle and accumulate a time series on the same standard.

## Criteria
With the scope subject to explanation and the minimum requirements of an explanation stated explicitly, it is PASS when **both the explanation-provision rate and user comprehension** are calculated, comprehension is measured by a **method that confirms understanding**, the two values are reported together, and a time series on the same standard is accumulated. It is judged FAIL if any one of the following applies — (a) the case in which the minimum requirements of an explanation were not fixed, so that the provision rate is no more than the distribution rate of boilerplate phrases, (b) the case in which comprehension was measured with satisfaction questions, so that whether it was understood is not confirmed, (c) the case in which only the provision rate is calculated and user comprehension is not produced. (a) and (b) leave only the appearance that an explanation was delivered, and (c) makes it fundamentally impossible to see the "provided but not understood" state this indicator seeks to catch, and each therefore runs counter to the purpose.
