# Political-policy resistance — Skill

## Purpose
Determine whether collective resistance corresponds to political-policy resistance, which seeks to control AI through regulation and institutions, and provide the grounds for a governance response based on the 'human-centered AI' philosophy.

## Input
- A record of the institutional channels of the target resistance (bills, regulatory debates, policy statements, etc.)
- Observed signs of issues (related to AI regulation, data sovereignty, restriction of use, and control)

## Procedure (Steps)
1. Confirm whether the resistance is raised through formal institutions such as legislative and regulatory bodies.
2. Confirm whether there is an issue of strengthening AI regulation or emphasizing data sovereignty.
3. Confirm whether there is an issue of restricting AI use or securing human control.
4. Synthesize the confirmed features and judge whether it is political-policy resistance.

## Output
The judgment result on whether the target reaction corresponds to political-policy resistance, and the priority order of the human-centered AI governance response.

## Criteria
When one or more of strengthening AI regulation, emphasizing data sovereignty, restricting AI use, and securing human control is confirmed, a PASS judgment (political-policy resistance) is made; when all are absent, a FAIL judgment (another type) is made.
