# Sociocultural resistance — Skill

## Purpose
Determine whether collective resistance corresponds to sociocultural resistance arising from cultural identity or distrust of the technological elite, and provide the grounds for a response of transparency and respect for creators.

## Input
- A record of the subject and form of the observed resistance (cultural movements, online discourse, consumer behavior, etc.)
- Signs of statements, movements, and perceptions related to the formation of an anti-AI culture or to distrust of the technological elite

## Procedure (Steps)
1. Confirm whether the subject of the resistance is a cultural group or discourse community rather than a particular organization.
2. Confirm signs of anti-AI culture formation such as human-first-ism, the Slow AI movement, and the emphasis on Human-made.
3. Confirm signs of the perception that "a few big tech firms control human society," that is, critiques of AI monopoly, data colonialism, and algorithmic power.
4. On the grounds of the confirmed sub-type, judge whether it is sociocultural resistance.

## Output
The judgment result on whether the target reaction corresponds to sociocultural resistance, its classification into a sub-type (formation of an anti-AI culture / distrust of the technological elite), and the priority order of the response.

## Criteria
When signs of one or more of the formation of an anti-AI culture and distrust of the technological elite are confirmed, a PASS judgment (sociocultural resistance) is made; when both types are absent, a FAIL judgment (another type) is made.
