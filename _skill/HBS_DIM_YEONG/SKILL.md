# Yeong (靈) — Skill

## Purpose
Determine whether, in decisions in the domain of yeong (dignity, morality, responsibility, agency), the right of judgment over whether ultimately to accept, reject, revise, or take responsibility remains with humans even when AI's analysis and proposals or robots' execution are used (whether yeong has been lost).

## Input
- Whether the target decision belongs to the domain of yeong (dignity, morality, responsibility, agency)
- The analysis, proposals, and execution results presented by AI and robots
- Whether procedures for final human approval and for the judgment of accepting/rejecting/revising/taking responsibility exist

## Procedure (Steps)
1. Check whether the target decision is in the domain of yeong, belonging to dignity, morality, responsibility, or agency — that is, whether it is a function that cannot be outsourced.
2. Grasp to what extent AI and robots are involved in that decision process.
3. Check whether there is a procedure in which humans ask themselves whether to accept, reject, revise, or take responsibility for the outputs of AI and robots.
4. Determine whether there are signs (loss of yeong) of acceptance without questioning, trust without verification, or evasion of responsibility.

## Output
The status of human retention of the final right of judgment in decisions in the domain of yeong (dignity, morality, responsibility, agency) and the determination result on whether yeong has been lost.

## Criteria
It is judged PASS when AI and robots are used while humans do not give up the last question (accept/reject/revise/take responsibility), and FAIL (loss of yeong) when the output is accepted as it is without questioning or responsibility is evaded.
