# Competence-Inferiority Type — Skill

## Purpose
It determines whether a member's backlash against AI adoption corresponds to the competence-inferiority type originating in the perception of one's own lack of competence relative to AI, and provides the grounds for responses that alleviate AI competence inequality.

## Input
- Records of remarks and behavior related to AI utilization of the target member or job group
- Observed signs of withdrawal (related to learning, digital utilization, self-deprecation, and generation)

## Procedure (Steps)
1. Check whether the target reaction appears in a situation where the gap in the speed of AI utilization is distinct (such as writing meeting reports).
2. Check for signs of learning fatigue or digital inferiority.
3. Check for signs of self-deprecation arising from differences in AI usage ability, or of the sense of technological alienation among middle-aged and older members.
4. Synthesize the confirmed characteristics and determine whether it is the competence-inferiority type.

## Output
The determination result of whether the target reaction corresponds to the competence-inferiority type, and the priority of responses to alleviate AI competence inequality.

## Criteria
If one or more of learning fatigue, digital inferiority, self-deprecation arising from differences in AI usage ability, and the sense of technological alienation among middle-aged and older members is confirmed, it is judged PASS (competence-inferiority type); otherwise it is judged FAIL (another type).
