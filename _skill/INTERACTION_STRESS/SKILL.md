# Relational (Interaction) Stress — Skill

## Purpose
Determine whether tension in the field of human-bot collaboration is not a problem internal to an individual actor but the stress of the relationship (interaction) itself, and thereby provide grounds for judging the need for intervention at the governance and organizational level.

## Input
- Observation records from the field of human-bot collaboration (descriptions relating to control, interpretation, responsibility, authority, and trust)
- The results of inspecting individual human and bot stress (comparison information for distinguishing it from relational stress)

## Procedure (Steps)
1. Distinguish whether the observed tension is a problem internal to the individual human or the individual bot, or a problem of the interaction between the two.
2. Confirm whether there is a feeling that the human cannot control the bot.
3. Confirm whether there are cases of the bot misinterpreting human intent.
4. Confirm whether there is an imbalance between human responsibility and AI authority.
5. Confirm whether human-AI trust is collapsing, and produce the result as whether it is relational stress.

## Output
Whether the observed tension is relational (interaction) stress and, where it is, its type (loss of a sense of control / misreading of intent / imbalance of responsibility and authority / collapse of trust).

## Criteria
If one or more of the four signs (loss of a sense of control, misreading of intent, imbalance of responsibility and authority, collapse of trust) are confirmed, judge PASS (relational stress, governance intervention required), and if none is confirmed, judge FAIL (not relational stress; re-inspect on the individual-stress axis).
