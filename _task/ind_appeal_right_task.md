# Right to Appeal — Task

## Tasks
1. Identify the intake routes for review requests — confirm through which desk requests come in and whether oral or informal submissions are left as records. If there is a route that is not recorded, the count will be lower than the actual figure.
2. Define the recognized scope of requests — state explicitly what will be counted as a "request to review an AI result" (objections to assessment, recommendation, allocation, or screening results).
3. Tally the **number of requests to review AI results** — record them divided by decision type and by department, and mark separately the ranges in which there was not a single request.
4. Do not interpret the number of requests on its own — read it alongside the existence and accessibility of the channel and the user understanding of the explainability indicator. A count of 0 has meaning only after it is judged whether it is "there are no objections" or "they could not be raised".
5. Classify the processing results — divide them into decision corrected, party concerned remedied, dismissed, and unprocessed or delayed.
6. Calculate the **correction/remedy processing rate** — compute the proportion of received requests that led to correction of the decision or to remedy. Dismissals and mere closures are not tallied as processing.
7. Record the time taken to process alongside — since even if a result comes out, if it is excessively late it cannot function as a remedy, state the period alongside.
8. Report the number of requests and the correction/remedy processing rate together, and fix a measurement cycle to accumulate a time series on the same standard.

## Deliverables
The number of review requests and the correction/remedy processing rate — the intake routes and whether they are recorded, the recognized scope of requests, the number of requests by decision type and by department and the ranges with no requests, the classification of processing results (correction, remedy, dismissal, unprocessed), the correction/remedy processing rate, the time taken to process, the grounds for interpreting the number of requests (checked against channel accessibility and understanding of explanations), the measurement cycle and the time-series record.
