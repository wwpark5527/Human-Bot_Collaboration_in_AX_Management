# Human Judgment Right — Task

## Tasks
1. Confirm the existence of the **list of tasks requiring human approval** — confirm whether the document exists, whether it is a finally approved version, and when its most recent revision date is, and record its location.
2. Record the scope of the list — write down how many task and decision types the list includes, and which output types are included and which are missing. Without a record of the scope, the number of omissions cannot be interpreted.
3. Secure the source materials for detecting omissions — confirm the materials that make it actually possible to find cases processed without approval, such as approval history, processing logs, and audit records. If there are no detection materials, a count of 0 omissions is recorded not as "there are no omissions" but as "undetectable".
4. Tally the **number of approval omissions** — count the cases among the tasks falling under the list that were completed or released without the required human approval, and leave alongside the task type and time of occurrence of each case.
5. Distinguish the types of omission — tally separately the cases where there was no approval at all, the cases approved by someone without authority, and the cases approved as a formality after the fact, so that the character of the control failure is exposed.
6. Report the scope of the list and the number of omissions together — since narrowing the list automatically reduces the number of omissions, do not read the two values separately.
7. Fix a measurement cycle and accumulate a time series on the same standard.

## Deliverables
The list of tasks requiring approval and the number of approval omissions — the location of the list and its most recent revision date, the scope of tasks and decisions the list covers, the source materials used for detecting omissions, the number of approval omissions and the breakdown by type, the marking of undetectable ranges, the measurement cycle and the time-series record.
